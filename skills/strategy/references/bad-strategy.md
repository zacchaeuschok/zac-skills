# The Four Hallmarks of Bad Strategy

Bad strategy is not the absence of strategy. It is the active avoidance of the hard work of crafting good strategy. Each hallmark is a specific, detectable failure.

---

## Hallmark 1: Fluff

Superficial abstraction using inflated vocabulary and buzzwords to mask the absence of thought.

**How to detect it:**
- Strip away all buzzwords. If the statement becomes meaningless or obvious, it is fluff.
- Ask "what is this actually saying?" If the answer is trivial or tautological, it is fluff.
- Apply the "duh" test — if the response is "duh, that's just routine business," it is fluff.

**Example:** A retail bank's strategy: "Our fundamental strategy is one of customer-centric intermediation." Translation: "Our strategy is being a bank." Intermediation — accepting deposits and lending — is literally what banks do. "Customer-centric" adds no substance.

**Common fluff words:** synergy, leverage (as verb), best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, stakeholder value, transformational, disruption, ecosystem, holistic, end-to-end, innovative, agile (when not referring to the methodology).

## Hallmark 2: Failure to face the challenge

The strategy does not identify or define the actual challenge. Without knowing the obstacle, no one can assess whether the strategy addresses it.

**How to detect it:**
- Ask "what specific problem are we solving?" If no clear answer exists, there is no strategy.
- The strategy glosses over obstacles or assumes positive thinking will prevail.
- It produces long lists of desirable outcomes without focus on what blocks progress.
- Resources are spread across many priorities rather than concentrated.

**Key insight:** "A strategy is a way through a difficulty, an approach to overcoming an obstacle, a response to a challenge." Without a named challenge, you have a stretch goal, a budget, or a wish list.

## Hallmark 3: Mistaking goals for strategy

Statements of desire are substituted for actual strategy. Ambition replaces analysis. The plan explains what success looks like but never how it will occur.

**How to detect it:**
- For any goal, demand: what competitive advantage or mechanism enables this outcome?
- Does the plan explain HOW success happens, or just assert that it WILL?
- Is it "long on goals and short on policy or action"?

**Example:** A CEO's "20/20 plan" — 20% annual revenue growth, 20% profit margin. When asked what competitive leverage enables this, his answer: "winning requires the will to win." Motivation does not replace strategic thinking.

**Common disguises:**
- "Our strategy is to grow revenue by 30%"
- "Our strategy is to be the market leader"
- "Our strategy is to create a world-class platform"
- "Our strategy is to delight customers"

Each states a desired outcome. None specifies an approach. None addresses a challenge.

## Hallmark 4: Bad strategic objectives

Objectives that fail to address critical challenges or are impractical. Two types:

**Dog's Dinner:** A long, unfocused list combining every stakeholder's wish, labeled "long-term" to defer accountability. One city produced 47 "strategies" and 178 action items. This is not strategy — it is a refusal to prioritize.

**Blue Sky:** Objectives as difficult as the original challenge. They provide no focus and no bridge to action. Requesting "transformational leadership" without diagnosing why current leaders struggle is a blue-sky objective — it restates the problem as its own solution.

**Test:** Can the team accomplish these objectives with existing resources and competencies? If the objectives are as hard as the original problem, the strategy has added no value.

---

## Why bad strategy persists

1. **Unwillingness to choose** — "Universal buy-in usually signals the absence of choice." Strategy requires saying no to someone.
2. **Template-style planning** — Filling in Vision / Mission / Values / Goals boxes without analysis.
3. **Positive-thinking culture** — Belief that focused desire produces results.
4. **Consensus compromise** — Avoiding hard choices to hurt no interests, which "cripples the whole."
