# The Kernel of Good Strategy

Good strategy has a logical structure called the kernel. It contains three elements. All three must be present. If any is missing, the strategy is incomplete at best, bad at worst.

---

## Diagnosis

The diagnosis defines the challenge. It simplifies overwhelming complexity into a coherent narrative that highlights what is critical and sets aside what is secondary.

**A good diagnosis:**
- Names the actual challenge, not symptoms or desired outcomes
- Identifies root causes — "underperformance" is a result, not a challenge
- Simplifies without obscuring — highlights the critical factors
- Is refutable — could be proven wrong by observable fact
- Leads to actionable implications — if no action follows, it is useless
- Often uses analogy, metaphor, or established frameworks
- Absorbs complexity so the organization receives a simpler, solvable problem

**A bad diagnosis:**
- No diagnosis at all (the most common failure)
- Restates desired outcomes instead of identifying what blocks them
- Focuses on symptoms rather than root causes
- Is too generic to distinguish this situation from any other
- Ignores existing constraints and obstacles
- Fails to provide a theory of why things have not worked

**Test:** Ask "what specific obstacle are we overcoming?" If the answer is "we need to grow" or "we need to be better," there is no diagnosis.

**Example:** IBM in 1993. Prevailing diagnosis: IBM is too integrated, should break up. Gerstner's diagnosis: IBM's problem is failing to use its integrated skills, not that it is integrated. Same facts. Completely different diagnosis. Completely different strategy.

## Guiding Policy

The guiding policy specifies the approach to overcoming the obstacle identified in the diagnosis. It is a directional marker — it channels action without fully defining implementation.

**An effective guiding policy:**
- Channels action in a specific direction
- Rules out other possible actions — implies difficult choices
- Creates advantage through anticipation, concentrated effort, or coherent policies
- Specifies the method for dealing with the challenge
- Could not be agreed to by everyone — someone would reasonably choose the opposite

**A bad guiding policy:**
- States goals, visions, or images of desirable end states
- Fails to rule out any actions — everything is still on the table
- Could apply to any organization in the industry
- Is so vague that no one could disagree with it
- Does not specify how to deal with the diagnosed challenge

**The opposite test:** If no reasonable competitor would choose the opposite, the guiding policy is too generic. "Focus on customer satisfaction" fails — who would choose to ignore customers? "Serve time-constrained professionals with premium convenience" passes — a competitor might reasonably choose to serve price-sensitive students instead.

**Test:** Ask "what are we saying no to?" If nothing is excluded, no choice has been made.

## Coherent Action

Coherent actions are specific, coordinated initiatives that execute the guiding policy. The word "coherent" is critical — actions must reinforce each other.

**What coherence means:**
- Resources, policies, and maneuvers are deliberately coordinated
- Each action makes the other actions more effective
- Coordination itself becomes a source of advantage
- All policies align with the central strategy

**What incoherence looks like:**
- Independent initiatives listed side by side
- Actions that compete for the same resources
- Internal contradictions (e.g., pursuing both brand premium and commodity pricing)
- No connection between actions — removing one would not affect the others

**Test:** Describe how each action reinforces another. If actions seem independent, they lack coherence.

**Example — IKEA:** Suburban locations enable large showrooms and parking. Catalogs replace sales staff. Flat-pack design reduces shipping and storage while enabling customer takeaway. No single element is proprietary. The integrated system has resisted replication for 50+ years because adopting only one element fails.
