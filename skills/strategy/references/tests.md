# Strategy Sniff Tests

Apply these tests to any strategy. Each produces a binary result — pass or fail. A strategy that fails multiple tests is almost certainly bad strategy.

---

## The Opposite Test

**Question:** Would a reasonable competitor choose the opposite?

If no one would reasonably choose the opposite, the statement is too generic to be strategic. It is a platitude, not a choice.

| Statement | Opposite | Strategic? |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| "Focus on customer satisfaction" | "Ignore customers" | No — no one would choose the opposite |
| "Serve time-constrained professionals with premium convenience" | "Serve price-sensitive students with budget options" | Yes — both are reasonable positions |
| "Pursue operational excellence" | "Pursue operational mediocrity" | No — tautological |
| "Win in Southeast Asia before expanding to Europe" | "Win in Europe before expanding to Southeast Asia" | Yes — both are defensible |

## The Bridge Test

**Question:** Does the strategy build a bridge between the challenge and action?

A strategy must connect what is hard (the diagnosis) to what we will do (the actions). When the objectives are as difficult as the original problem, no bridge exists. The strategy has restated the problem in different words.

**Fail example:** "Our challenge is low retention. Our strategy is to improve retention." The objective is as hard as the problem. No bridge.

**Pass example:** "Our challenge is low retention driven by poor onboarding. Our policy is to front-load value delivery in the first 48 hours. We will redesign the activation flow to reach first value in under 10 minutes." The bridge connects diagnosis → policy → action.

## The No Test

**Question:** What does this strategy say "no" to?

Strategy is choice. Choice means exclusion. If the strategy does not explicitly or implicitly exclude actions, resources, markets, or customers, no real choice has been made.

**Fail signals:**
- All existing initiatives continue
- Every business unit's requests are accommodated
- No budget was cut, no project was cancelled
- The plan is additive — everything old plus everything new

**Pass signals:**
- Specific markets, segments, or products are deprioritized
- Resources are reallocated from one area to another
- Some teams or initiatives are explicitly shut down
- The strategy names what the organization will stop doing

"Universal buy-in usually signals the absence of choice."

## The Simplicity Test

**Question:** Does the strategy look simple and obvious in hindsight?

Good strategy almost always looks simple once articulated. Complexity typically masks weak thinking rather than reflecting genuine difficulty. If the strategy requires a 50-page deck to explain, the thinking is not clear.

**Caveat:** Simple does not mean easy. The diagnosis may be hard to reach. The actions may be hard to execute. But the logic connecting them should be clear.

## The Coherence Test

**Question:** Do the proposed actions reinforce each other?

List every proposed action. For each pair, ask: does action A make action B more effective? If most actions are independent — they neither help nor hinder each other — the strategy lacks coherence. It is a to-do list, not a strategy.

**Pass:** IKEA's actions reinforce each other. Suburban locations enable large showrooms. Flat-pack enables customer transport. Catalogs replace sales staff. Each element makes the others work better.

**Fail:** "Launch a mobile app AND expand into enterprise AND open a physical store AND hire a data science team." Four independent initiatives. No reinforcement.

## The Focus Test

**Question:** Does the strategy specify what the organization will NOT do?

Closely related to the No Test, but focused on ongoing constraint rather than one-time exclusion. A focused strategy maintains constraints over time. It resists the natural organizational tendency to add initiatives, serve more segments, and dilute resources.

**Test:** Revisit the strategy in six months. If three new priorities have been added and none removed, focus has been lost.

---

## Diagnostic Questions

When the sniff tests produce ambiguous results, these questions sharpen the evaluation:

1. What is the fundamental challenge? Is it clearly stated?
2. Why has this not been solved before? Is there a theory of prior failure?
3. What specific obstacles block progress? Root causes, not symptoms.
4. Where does the organization have asymmetric advantages?
5. What constraints are accepted? What will we not do?
6. How does each action reinforce the others?
7. Is this grounded in evidence or just brainstorming?
8. Does the strategy make leadership uncomfortable but confident? If no one is uncomfortable, no real choices were made.
