# Writing Rules

These rules apply to the final output. They are non-negotiable.

---

## Structure

**Lead with the conclusion.** The first sentence of every section is the answer. The reader should know the point before seeing the evidence. Never build up to the point.

**Headings are verdicts.** A heading states what is true, not what the section discusses. Wrong: "Revenue." Right: "Revenue grew 20% driven by enterprise expansion." The reader should be able to read only the headings and understand the full argument.

**One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph serves two purposes, split it. A paragraph is a unit of thought, not a unit of length.

**Short paragraphs.** Maximum four sentences. White space is not wasted space — it is structure. Dense blocks of text signal unclear thinking.

## Sentences

**Maximum 25 words per sentence.** If a sentence needs a semicolon, it is two sentences. If a sentence has more than one comma-separated clause, it is probably two sentences.

**Active voice.** "The team shipped the feature" not "The feature was shipped by the team." Passive voice hides the actor. Name the actor.

**Specific over general.** "Revenue grew 20%" not "Revenue grew significantly." "Three customers churned" not "Some customers churned." Numbers over adjectives.

## Inventory and Data

**Tables for three or more comparable items.** Never enumerate more than two items inside a sentence. Three or more items belong in a table or a bullet list.

**Data before interpretation.** Present the number, then state what it means. "Churn rose to 8% — double the industry average. The retention program is not working."

## What to Cut

**No hedge words.** Delete: "somewhat," "arguably," "notably," "it is worth noting," "it could be said that," "potentially," "in some sense." These words exist because the writer is not sure. Decide, then state.

**No generic transitions.** Delete: "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Moreover," "In addition," "It should also be noted." These mean "I could not find a real connection between these paragraphs." Connect on substance — carry a word or concept from the end of one section into the beginning of the next.

**No throat-clearing.** Delete: "As mentioned above," "It is important to note that," "As we discussed," "The purpose of this section is to." Start with the point.

**No filler conclusions.** Delete: "In conclusion," "To summarize," "In summary." If the document is structured correctly, the conclusion is obvious from the pyramid. If it is not obvious, the structure is broken — fix the structure, not the conclusion.
