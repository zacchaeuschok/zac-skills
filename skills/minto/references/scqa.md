# SCQA Framework

The introduction to any structured document follows four components. Their order changes with tone, but all four must be present.

---

## Situation

A statement the reader already knows and agrees with. It anchors the reader in shared reality. No new information. No controversy.

The situation establishes time and place. It answers: "what is the stable state the reader recognizes?"

**Test:** if the reader would say "I didn't know that," it is not a situation — it is a complication or evidence.

Examples:
- "We launched the self-service portal in Q3 to reduce support volume."
- "The board approved a $5M expansion into Indonesia last year."
- "Our API serves 12M requests per day with 99.9% uptime."

## Complication

What changed, went wrong, or threatens the situation. The complication creates tension. It makes the reader ask "so what do we do?"

A complication is one of:
- Something went wrong
- Something could go wrong
- Something changed
- Something could change
- An alternative viewpoint emerged
- A decision must be made between options

**Test:** if removing the complication leaves no reason to write the document, it is the right complication.

Examples:
- "Support volume increased 30% despite the portal — users cannot find their billing history."
- "The Indonesia market contracted 8% and our local partner defaulted."
- "A competitor launched a free tier that undercuts our pricing by 60%."

## Question

The question the complication raises in the reader's mind. State it explicitly. The reader should think "yes, that is exactly what I want to know."

The question is always one of:
- What should we do?
- How do we do it?
- Why did this happen?
- Which option should we choose?

**Test:** the answer to this question must be the governing thought. If it is not, the question is wrong.

## Answer

The governing thought. One sentence. This is the apex of the pyramid.

The answer must directly respond to the question. If the reader asks "what should we do?" the answer must be an action, not a description.

---

## Tone Arrangements

### Direct (default)

Lead with the answer. Use when the reader trusts you or needs the conclusion immediately.

> **[Answer].** [Situation]. [Complication].

### Considered

Build the case before revealing the answer. Use when the reader needs context before they can accept the conclusion.

> [Situation]. [Complication]. [Question]. **[Answer].**

### Concerned

Lead with the problem. Use when the reader does not yet know there is a problem.

> [Complication]. [Situation]. **[Answer].**
